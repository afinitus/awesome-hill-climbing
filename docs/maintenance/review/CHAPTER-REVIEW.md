## Findings and fixes

### Major: Q-chunking backed up a different selection policy

`algos/05_rlpd_qchunk.py`, `Learner.update`: acting selected `argmax(mean(online critics))`, while the TD target selected `argmax(min(random pair of target critics))`. These can choose different chunks. The chapter disclosed the difference as a more cautious policy, but that contradicts the requested same-policy backup and the cited QC formulation. [Q-chunking Appendix B.4, equations 23–24](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07969v1#A2.SS4) uses the implicit acting policy to generate the target action.

Changed selection to the online ensemble mean, then evaluated that same chunk using the target-pair minimum. Regression tests use intentionally opposite online/target rankings and an online tie; both fail under the previous target. The full original configuration, all four methods and all three seeds, was rerun successfully in 46.619 wall minutes with `uv run python algos/05_rlpd_qchunk.py --out runs/prelaunch_ch05`. Its results, chapter/index text, three plots and GIF are synchronized. No result number was edited to simulate a rerun.

Corrected QC scores 238, 245 and 238 of 256, pooled **721/768 = 93.9% [92.0, 95.4]**, replacing 714/768. Corrected QC-CQL scores 154, 140 and 141, pooled **435/768 = 56.6% [53.1, 60.1]**, replacing 268/768. Its former late-collapse/below-base conclusion is not supported by the corrected run and has been retired. Every SAC/RLPD seed and the random-pick control reproduced its original final count. The QC seed-1 point estimate exceeds 95%; the pooled headline remains below 95%.

The [archive manifest](archive/ch05_target_policy_bug/manifest.json) preserves all thirteen superseded JSONs and all thirteen raw corrected JSONs before accounting. The active `results/ch05/` contains only the thirteen corrected replacements. The superseded full run contributes a named development charge of 572,459 training steps (954.098 robot-minutes), after removing duplicated pilot/shared-data charges, and 4,999 demo steps (8.332 human-minutes). No new search charge is added: the old search fields contained only previously charged pilots. Historical evaluation remains diagnostic cost, excluded from improvement cost. Fresh QC training costs are 95.85, 101.02 and 96.71 robot-minutes; cumulative train-plus-search costs are 1,443.29, 1,448.46 and 1,444.15. Learning-curve axes explicitly show fresh costs. `make leaderboard` rebuilt both plots and the 145-row README table; its note distinguishes recorded init sets and exploratory/ablation rows from chapter headlines.

Rerun provenance needs the reviewed source and archive manifest, not the result JSON's `git_sha` alone. Ledger records HEAD at write time and does not capture uncommitted edits; the corrected selector was already present as an uncommitted edit when training began. Raw `git_sha` fields are preserved. Subsequent Chapter 5 source changes were historical-cost constants/metadata and their comments, the fresh-cost plot label, and documentation comments; training and policy-selection semantics did not change during the run. ### Major: smoke tests exposed held-out data during development

Every chapter except Chapter 6 used held-out `eval` episodes during `--quick`; Chapter 7 also used `stress`. Chapter 3 explicitly reports a method choice after a quick-mode peek. Although quick runs wrote outside the public result folders, this still made the held-out outcomes available while developing the method.

Changed quick-mode final evaluations and their diagnostic/media replays to `search`. Full-run datasets and existing measurements are unchanged. Chapter 6 already had this protection. The historical Chapter 3 peek remains disclosed; the patch cannot undo it. Tagged the legacy aggregate row as exploratory for the leaderboard and relabeled ambiguous narrative references. The public Chapter 3 lead already uses the predeclared-method result, `racing` seed 1, 219/256; clarified that the saved `golden_ticket` row and its internal `headline` key refer to the exploratory 224/256 result. ### Major: finite failures were described as absence of policy support

`docs/chapters/ch07.md` called the 54 `stress` states that failed 16 Gaussian trials states with “no support” and concluded residual gains came from outside the base's support. The chapter's own next independent Gaussian trial succeeds on 2/54 of them. Finite failed samples cannot demonstrate zero success probability or absence of a successful sequence of base chunks.

Reworded the lead, support diagnostic and chapter index to describe the measured difficult-state groups. Preserved every count and interval. Also removed a universal statement that *any* fixed state-to-noise map helps: the evidence covers three sampled maps. The existing evidence is useful but does not prove a support boundary.

### Minor: residual TD3 attribution and backup caveat

`docs/chapters/ch06.md` grouped its three-step target with TD3's original mechanisms and described itself as the first chapter that trains with RL despite Chapter 5. [TD3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09477) uses a one-step target; this implementation adds a three-step replay return without importance correction. Added the off-policy approximation caveat and corrected the chapter ordering prose. No experiment changed.

## Algorithm and isolation checks

- Chapter 0: flow-matching training uses the straight-path velocity target; calibration cells, chosen dataset and base seed are chosen on `search`. Demo pools are independent of the named evaluation seed sets. Deliberate under-training and the same env/controller RNG seed caveat remain disclosed.
- Chapter 1: antithetic FD includes its documented factor-of-two step-size convention; ARS ranks mirrored pairs by their better return and divides by the standard deviation of the selected `2b` returns, matching [ARS Algorithm 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.07055v1#S3). The zero-spread branch avoids division by zero. All candidate and monitor rollouts are charged. Final iterate selection uses search counts with a documented tie rule.
- Chapter 2: CMA-ES includes both evolution paths, inverse-square-root whitening, the `h_sigma` correction, rank-one/rank-mu covariance updates and exponential step-size adaptation. Compared with [Hansen's tutorial](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.00772v2#A1). Positive-only recombination, tie sharing and clipping repaired candidates are course adaptations, not the full active-CMA algorithm. PI² uses normalized exponential terminal costs; this is the chapter's parameter-search specialization, not a reproduction of time-varying trajectory PI². The source [PI² paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/theodorou10a.html) and chapter explain the distinction. BO/Sobol select only using search trials and search audits.
- Chapter 3: racing ranks cumulative successes on shared, progressively extended subsets of search states. The 512-ticket schedule spends 512 + 6 × 256 = 2,048 episodes. The winner among methods is selected by search score; the eval-selected diagnostic is labeled optimistic. All pooled ticket evals and support probes are diagnostics, not policy selection. Historical peeking remains a limitation.
- Chapter 5: chunk targets discount rewards within the four executed actions and bootstrap with `gamma**4`; terminal/time-limit masks prevent bootstrapping from an ended finite-horizon episode. The fixed target now selects the same chunk as acting. SAC/RLPD omit the entropy term from the critic backup by design; they are modified baselines, not faithful reproductions of the original algorithms. This remains disclosed.
- Chapter 6: residual mean starts at exactly zero; exploration and target smoothing are clipped before scaling by the residual bound; the critic trains on the executed summed action. Target critics/actor use Polyak updates and actor updates are delayed. Three-step off-policy approximation is now explicit.
- Chapter 7: replay records the executed latent, bounded noise is decoded by the frozen base, and the PSS basis comes from training-state network probes. Every network initialization is seeded. The published recipe uses a Monte Carlo critic over behavior-policy replay and a fixed temperature, not DSRL's original SAC TD recipe; the chapter already explains that distinction. Checkpoint selection includes the untrained zero-ticket fallback and uses search only.
- Chapter 8: the target selects with the online critic mean and values the same candidate with a target-pair minimum. Replay uses executed chunks, episode ends are masked, editor output is bounded, and beta selection pools final search scores. Its chunk reward is the undiscounted within-chunk success flag with elapsed-step discount between chunks, an explicit convention in the code and calibration analysis; it is not the same numeric return as discounting the reward at each internal step. Co-training uses successful episodes only and is disclosed as a departure from EXPO.
- Chapter 12: verifier validation is split by complete training episode, so decisions carrying the same outcome label never straddle training and validation. Verifier type and N are selected on search, pooled over seeds; held-out eval is used afterwards. All label collection, sweeps, oracle continuations and supplied pilot costs are charged. The stored proposal includes its unexecuted planned suffix, but that suffix is produced before the outcome and is not a future label leak.

Default environment training/demo seed ranges: Chapter 0, 50,000+ (2,000 per pipeline seed; sloppy pool +1,000); Chapter 12, 70,000+ (10,000 per verifier seed); Chapter 7, 110,000+ (hard +50,000, seed +10,000; PSS probes +9,000); Chapter 8, 200,000+ (100,000 per seed); Chapter 6, 1,000,000+ (50,000 per run); Chapter 5, 5,000,000+ (1,000,000 per seed; online +100,000, demos +900,000/+950,000). None overlaps search 10,000–10,063, eval 20,000–20,255, eval_ext 30,000–31,023 or stress 40,000–40,255 for the published configurations. Arbitrarily large/negative custom CLI seeds and budgets are not globally range-validated.

## Statistical verification and exact replay

The checked-in audit command is `uv run python docs/maintenance/review/verify_chapter_results.py --replay`. It writes neither results nor media. It traverses all committed result files, re-derives rates and Wilson intervals from k/n, and recomputes McNemar tests from saved discordant counts. It also checks explicitly written `k/n = percentage [interval]` statements in chapter Markdown. Repeated shared analyses in result JSONs contribute repeated checks; the object count is not a count of independent experiments.

Before the Chapter 5 correction, all 145 result JSONs passed: 17,669 nested count objects, 232 explicit document count/percentage statements, and 220 associated intervals had no arithmetic discrepancy. The paired-test traversal re-derived 181 discordant-count objects plus 666 nested paired objects (847 checks, including duplicated analyses). Three Chapter 5 Fisher tests also match the text: 0.014532, 0.198106 and 0.042210. The automated document parser does not cover implicit denominators in table headers, arbitrary prose claims or every plot label; these require the chapter/result reconciliation described here.

An independent exact convolution of each empirical paired-difference distribution also checks the saved bootstrap intervals: 666 interval objects, representing 38 distinct `(fixed, broke, n)` distributions before the correction. Every endpoint is within one episode-count increment of the exact resampling quantile, consistent with the saved algorithm's 10,000 Monte Carlo draws. This checks the interval calculation, not bitwise regeneration of its random draws; ordered outcome vectors are not saved for every chapter.

After publishing the correction, `uv run python docs/maintenance/review/verify_chapter_results.py` passes with 145 result files, 17,669 count objects, 181 discordant tests, 666 nested paired tests, 666 bootstrap objects (38 distinct distributions; largest endpoint gap one count), 233 explicit Markdown rates and 224 associated intervals. The [publication check](CH05-PUBLICATION-CHECK.json) confirms all 26 archive hashes, the source and both checkpoint hashes, exactly thirteen active corrected rows, unchanged outcome/curve/statistic/configuration/`git_sha`/timestamp fields versus the raw rerun, and only the declared budget additions. All three Chapter 5 plots and both leaderboard plots were visually inspected; the regenerated GIF contains 51 frames.

Durable output: [arithmetic verifier](CHAPTER-VERIFICATION.txt), [corrected full training log](archive/ch05_target_policy_bug/corrected_training.log), and the archive manifest above. ## Scope limits and launch status

The shared environment's physical anti-exploit check is the main review's responsibility. These chapter scores measure the existing CupDrop-v1 success definition; they must not be represented as evidence that no policy moves or rights the cup. A separate replay of the corrected QC seed-0 critic reproduced 238/256 and found three successful episodes with final cup displacement >1 cm (20068, 20151, 20183). No successful episode had its cube center below the cup floor while horizontally inside. Maximum sampled cube contact penetration across all episodes was 5.33 mm; sampling control-step boundaries is not an exhaustive physics check. This diagnostic consumed 10,481 env steps (17.47 robot-minutes), outside policy selection. Evidence and checkpoint hash: [QC replay probe](QC-REPLAY-PROBE.json); command: `uv run python docs/maintenance/review/replay_corrected_qc.py`, using the committed `checkpoints/ch05_qc_s0_review.npz` (SHA-256 recorded in the probe).
