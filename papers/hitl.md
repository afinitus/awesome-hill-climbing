# Human corrections & DAgger

[← Awesome Hill Climbing](../README.md) · [all families](README.md)

*My imitation policy works most of the time but fails in states the demos never covered. How do I spend a small amount of human time so the next version fixes exactly those failures?*

Run the policy, let a person (or now a machine) step in where it goes wrong, and train on those on-policy fixes so the policy learns to handle the states it actually reaches.

- **Loop step:** Find failures → Improve
- **Built on:** Compounding error (covariate shift); DAgger: label the learner's own states; Human-gated vs robot-gated takeovers; Reweighting correction data; A takeover as a reward, value or preference signal; Human effort is the budget
- **Use it when:** Use this family when you have a decent imitation policy (roughly 30% to 80% success) that fails in specific, repeatable places, and a person who can recognize and fix those failures. It is the default last-mile tool when you have no reward function, when the task is long-horizon or contact-rich, and when you want the policy to stay close to its pretrained behavior.
- **What changed in 2025–26:** Over the last 12 months, the policy being corrected became a VLA: pi0/pi0.5-class models replace small BC-RNNs, and the loops are built as systems. SOP streams fleet corrections to a cloud learner, and HELP splits the human work between a teleoperator and a floor operator who together supervise twelve robots. Methods also get more out of each correction: FlowPRO trains on paired failed and corrected segments with a DPO-style loss, and FlowDAgger maps corrections into a frozen flow policy's noise space instead of retraining it. Machines increasingly take the intervener's role (UniIntervene, Skill-Space Shooting), and how and when a correction is collected is now studied directly (RaC's recover-then-correct rule, TimelyDAgger's takeover timing).

- **[IWR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06733)** (Stanford; UT Austin, 2020-12-12) — Balanced sampling of intervention vs robot data; Threading final 87.3±5.0 vs HG-DAgger 75.3±8.1 and Full Demos 76.7±2.3 (sim).
- **[Learning from Interventions](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss16/p055.html)** (Princeton; University of Washington, 2020-07) — Expert Intervention Learning (EIL) uses both interventions and non-interventions as feedback on state quality and action optimality; learns collision avoidance from about one minute of expert control.
- **[HG-DAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02890)** (Stanford University; UIUC, 2018-10-05) — Human takeovers supply labels for iterative imitation learning. In the real-car test’s first 5,000 samples, HG-DAgger records no collisions or road departures, versus DAgger’s one each and BC’s one collision and six departures.
- **[EnsembleDAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08364)** (Menda et al., 2018-07-22) — DAgger extension using neural-network ensemble variance as a confidence measure to decide when the novice acts versus the expert; tested on inverted pendulum and MuJoCo HalfCheetah (IROS 2019).
- **[DART](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09327)** (Laskey et al., 2017-03-27) — Injects optimized noise into the supervisor's policy during demonstration to collect recovery data, an off-policy alternative to DAgger; 62% gain over behavior cloning on real robot grasping.
- **[Query-Efficient Imitation Learning for End-to-End Autonomous Driving (SafeDAgger)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06450)** (Zhang et al., 2016-05-20) — SafeDAgger extends DAgger with a safety policy predicting when the learner deviates from the reference, cutting expert queries; converges faster in a car racing simulator.
- **[DAgger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)** (Carnegie Mellon University, 2010-11-02) — Aggregates expert labels on the learner’s own states and retrains. In Super Tux Kart, the policy almost never fell off after five iterations, while supervised behavior cloning did not improve with more expert data.
