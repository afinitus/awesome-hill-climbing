# World models & evaluation

[← Awesome Hill Climbing](../README.md) · [all families](README.md)

*How do I tell whether checkpoint k+1 really beats checkpoint k, and how do I improve a policy, when every real robot trial is slow, noisy and needs a reset?*

Build a stand-in for the real robot, either a learned video or latent world model or a scanned real-to-sim twin, then use it to score checkpoints cheaply and to run the try-score-improve loop without spending robot time.

- **Loop step:** Deploy → Measure
- **Built on:** Dyna: learn a model, then practice inside it; Latent imagination (PlaNet/Dreamer); Monte Carlo policy evaluation in a stand-in world; Model exploitation and compounding error; Rewards inside imagination; Real-to-sim twins; Runtime monitors
- **Use it when:** Use evaluation infrastructure first, almost always. If you will compare more than a handful of checkpoints, build a twin or learned evaluator and validate it on 5-10 checkpoints with paired real trials (report Pearson r and MMRV) before trusting it.
- **What changed in 2025–26:** In the last 12 months the family went from 'world models as evaluators' to 'world models as RL environments for VLAs', and then to 'making those environments trustworthy'. From September to November 2025, World-Env, VLA-RFT and WMPO ran GRPO/PPO-style RL for VLAs inside learned world models, scored by a VLM judge, a learned success classifier or similarity to a reference rollout; the draw was on-policy RL without resets. By February-March 2026 the focus had shifted to hallucination and compounding error: WoVR (keyframe-initialized rollouts, model-policy co-evolution), VLAW and PlayWorld (grounding the model in real policy rollouts or failure-rich play data) and VLA-MBPO (short imagined branches) each target ways an imperfect world model misleads the policy. On real robots, RISE reports 30-35% -> 85-95% (20 trials per task) and WoVR 51.1 -> 80.0 on a Franka, and evaluators were validated at larger scale (Veo against 1600+ real trials; RoboWorld at r = 0.989 with the RoboArena leaderboard).

- **[Beyond Conservatism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33336)** (Chen et al., 2026-09-27) — RECON: model-based IL splitting a conservative main policy from an explorer that seeks epistemically uncertain yet recoverable states (via multi-step imagination); improves interaction efficiency on locomotion, navigation, manipulation.
